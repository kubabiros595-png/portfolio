Clinical Trials Monitor

Weekly research digest from ClinicalTrials.gov and PubMed

Built and tested on public data, not deployed. Public data from ClinicalTrials.gov and PubMed (U.S. National Library of Medicine). Not medical advice; summaries were not reviewed by a medical professional.

  • n8n
  • Public APIs
  • Life sciences
  • Claude

11 of 11 rule-filter decisions correct; 4 of 4 planted mistakes caught.

My role. I built this project with AI assistance. I chose the sources and the topic, defined the rules for what is in scope, the checks and the forbidden wording, set the expected answers for every test record, designed the canaries, and reviewed the summaries and the fixes.

Problem

Medical, clinical operations and market access teams need to know what changed in their therapy area: new trials, trials that started recruiting or stopped, new results and reviews.

Checking two databases by hand every week is slow and it is easy to misread a status. An AI summary helps, but a wrong phase, a wrong trial number or a sentence that sounds like advice is a real risk.

How it works

  • Rule in code
  • AI step
  • Person decides
  • Input or storage
  1. InputEvery Monday: topic and look-back window from one config node
  2. InputRead-only requests to ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 and PubMed E-utilities
    • API fails after retries → error logged, the other source still runs
  3. Rule (code)One record format; skip records already seen
  4. Rule (code)Rule filter: required fields, English, status, phase, publication type
    • reject → saved with the reason
  5. AI (Claude)Claude writes a plain-language JSON summary from the record's own fields
  6. Rule (code)Code checks: link matches the record, phase and status match the source, no dosing or advice
    • fail → saved with a FLAG, shown first
  7. PersonReview form: approve or reject with a note

What did not pass the first time, and what I changed

First full evaluation did not pass: 6 of 7 summaries matched on phase and status.

Claude wrote that a trial “has not yet started recruiting”, which is correct, and my status check saw the word “recruiting”. The check now ignores the word after “not”, “yet”, “never” or “no longer”. I emptied the tables and ran the full test again.

Device and behavioural trials were rejected.

The first rule allowed only drug phases, so trials of an app or a stimulation method were dropped. Interventional trials without a phase are now allowed; observational studies are still rejected.

A correct phase was flagged.

A summary named a phase from the abstract while the record's phase field was empty. A phase in the text may now come from the source fields, title or abstract; the phase field itself must still match exactly.

Limitations

  • Built and tested, not deployed; not medical advice.
  • One topic and a small number of records per week; topics and filters would be agreed with a medical or regulatory team.
  • The checks catch the mistakes they are written for. A summary can still mislead in a way no rule detects, which is why every summary waits for review.
  • The repository stores record IDs, titles and AI summaries, but not the PubMed abstracts, which may be protected by copyright.
